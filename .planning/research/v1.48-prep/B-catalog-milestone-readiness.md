## Options

### Option 1: catalog as planned (five phases, about 11 plans)
Pros: matches the approved roadmap and the maintainer's stated review surface (browsable tree, playground, search); work is sequenced by dependency; every phase already has a defined output (A-scope table). Cons: the plan was written before v1.47 changed the base. It double-counts the copy layer, treats 196 as scheduled work, and has no stop rule if the spike numbers are bad. Risk: eleven plans of mostly evidence-flavoured output can trip the progress guard (second consecutive evidence-only plan forces a stop). Verdict: close, not as written.

### Option 2: trimmed spike only (192 plus a one-screen proof)
Pros: smallest cost (2 plans), answers the only irreversible question (dependency, NIF, CSS isolation, CI delta), keeps the maintainer free to choose adopter features next. Cons: produces no visible or checkable product value on its own, because a spike without fixtures and a lint hosts no executable check that the repo lacks today; the v1.50 screens work then re-asks the same cost question; extraction (the lasting value, area components over pure view-models) is deferred again. Under the zero-human-review rule, a catalog nobody can browse-and-judge is only worth the checks it hosts, so a spike alone proves feasibility but delivers no check. Verdict: right as Phase 1 of option 1, wrong as the whole milestone.

### Option 3: defer the catalog; do adopter features (library) next
Pros: adopter value is the project's core (Hex library; ops is not on Hex). The testing kit, `search_indexable?/1`, highlighting, limits and backfill tasks are in `lib/`, and the roadmap says to grill API shapes first. No new dependency tree, no CI cost. Cons: the five adopter features sit on a Search screen the owner plans to extract (the earlier plan said highlighting should land on a composable, storied Search surface); the UI engine keeps a stale `phase173` fixture mode in production `PostureLive`; the copy table's own revisit trigger points at the lint. It also leaves ops UI quality guarded by 10 hand-built gates with no state coverage. Verdict: a legitimate maintainer choice, not an error; it is what to pick if the spike scares the maintainer. See the adversarial pass.

## What is verifiable by executable check, and what is not

Executable (all runnable locally, none needs a human):
- every variation renders with no warning or raise (`render_component` or `rendered_to_string`, public LiveView API (K)); every `OpsUi` component (declared through `attr`, enumerable via `__components__/0` (K)) has a story or a written exemption; no two variations render identical HTML; every variation cites twin, persona and `JOB-*`;
- fixture invariants; the rendered-text lint; `decide/4` branch table; the JOB-03 journey (status word beside icon, one primary action);
- boundary and route absence in prod and test; dependency and NIF recorded numbers; package must-not-contain `fixtures/`;
- structural before/after pairs for each extraction using `scripts/ci/ui_state_pairs.cjs` (R); axe, overflow and target-size in a browser, advisory until clean for 14 nights.

Not verifiable by machine, and not claimed: whether a story is the right state to show; whether the extraction looks better; hierarchy and voice. Per the repo rule these are batched maintainer decisions with before/after links, never a UAT step and never described as reviewed. The audit-style closing line stays: the checks are not independent review and not aesthetic approval.

## Lens notes (what moved the recommendation)

- Architecture. Keep the boundary where it is: catalog in `scrypath_ops` (not a Hex package, `scrypath_ops/mix.exs` has no `package:`), story files outside `lib/`, area components inside `lib/` with narrow `attr` interfaces over pure view-model builders (deep modules: a small interface, the Repo, async and URL concerns hidden in the LiveView). `decide/4` is the deep module of the pilot: one pure function, closed outcome type, called by the LiveView and by stories alike. Anti-pattern: stories of states the builder cannot produce; mocks of Repo or `Scrypath` in stories (RECOMMENDATIONS "Antipatterns to refuse"). Footgun: two rendering paths. v1.47's `CopySurfaces` renders through real LiveViews (`live(conn, "/ops")` with env stubs); stories render components. Keep both, feed both into one table, and do not migrate `CopySurfaces` to stories in this milestone: it tests the mount path, which stories do not.
- Dependency footprint. storybook adds 9 packages (+17 percent of the 54-package lock) including one Rust NIF (`mdex_native`, precompiled and checksum-verified, fetched at compile time) (R, A-scope facts 11 and 13). Against house preference (copy-paste over a dependency), ADR 0001 already accepts it, and ops is unpublished. The fallback harness is real and bounded; the spike is where the preference is re-tested with numbers. One cost the spike must price: `ops_dependency_boundary_test.exs` compiles fresh dependency graphs, so the NIF download recurs unless `RUSTLER_PRECOMPILED_GLOBAL_CACHE_PATH` is cached (K, rustler_precompiled docs); BEAM caches go cold every 1 to 2 weeks (V1.46-PREP D12: `repository-contracts` 775 s cold versus 19 s warm).
- Security. The only new exposure is the playground, which evaluates templates on the server; the 2026 RCE and atom-exhaustion CVEs were fixed in 1.1.0 and 1.5.0 is not affected (V1.46-PREP D12, R). Risk is deployment shape. Dev endpoint is loopback (`config/dev.exs:21`) with `check_origin: false`; inside a Compose service the bind must be 0.0.0.0 published as `127.0.0.1:PORT`, so document and assert that. Recommended deviation from earlier notes: mount the route in dev only, not test, and have the sweep run against a dev-env Compose service; the test environment compiles story data for ExUnit but exposes no route. Task-scoped Docker names and cleanup per AGENTS.md apply.
- DevOps and CI. PR hosted CI is about 16 minutes, dominated by `ecommerce-mounted` (1,134 to 1,186 s cold), not `ops-ui` (about 386 s, path-scoped; CI-EFFICIENCY). The catalog's PR-time tests are ExUnit inside `ops-ui` (no browser), so the critical path should not lengthen; the cost is cold-cache time and the NIF. The browser sweep stays nightly and advisory (D5 of RECOMMENDATIONS): no new hosted browser lane on PRs, DB-free stories need no Docker on PRs. Record measured lane times in `.planning/reference/CI-EFFICIENCY.md` after the spike. Gate budget 10 of 12: plan for net +1 (fixtures) and +1 (JOB-03), with the lint and catalog contract replacing rows; if both replacements slip, stop at 12 and defer anything else.
- Design and UX. The catalog is the first place the maintainer can browse states (payoff stated in A-scope). Keep extraction pixel-identical so it stays outside the visible-UI approval path; copy and design fixes found during stories are batched for one yes. Use the preview refresh and `examples/scrypath_ecommerce/e2e/UI-REVIEW.md` (full inventory before narrowing; captures and passing tests are not a design pass).
- Accessibility. Primitives rendered alone make attributable axe results (WCAG 2.2 AA tags; `target-size` 24 px). The copy table's `not_covered` list admits aria-label, title and placeholder are unguarded; accessible names are best checked by axe in a browser, so that gap closes only in the advisory sweep. Honest limit: no real screen reader coverage.
- Visual regression. Pixel baselines were the Phase 185 pain (timestamps, border raster, font reset; UI-REVIEW.md). Do not add hosted pixel baselines now. Use structural pairs locally for extraction, element screenshots only after a pinned Linux baseline exists (RECOMMENDATIONS D6), and never auto-update a baseline.
- OSS maintainer. Bus factor of one on phoenix_storybook (cblavier, about 877 commits of the total (R, storybook research)); steady cadence (1.0.0 March to 1.5.0 September, nothing since September, repo commit 2026-11-02). Mitigation is the fallback and the unpublished boundary. Other ecosystems' lesson (K): component catalogs rot when stories are hand-curated prop permutations and nothing fails when a component changes; the durable form makes stories the input to tests (Storybook's portable stories, Playwright and axe over stories, GOV.UK and Primer style states-over-permutations). Here the equivalent is the render-all gate and the manifest staleness check.

## Anti-patterns and footguns to carry into planning
- Prop-permutation stories; stories without a citation; a second inventory page beside the stories.
- `.story.exs` files sit outside `mix compile --warnings-as-errors`, so attr typos surface at render time (K, storybook guide). The render-all ExUnit gate is the compensating check, so it must exist before the first story PR merges.
- A mirrored `storybook.css` that is not an `@import` of `app.css` drifts (ADR 0001 choice; keep the import).
- Counting a passing retry as green; auto-updating a baseline; a model judge as a CI gate (conflicts with the no-recurring-model-gate rule).
- Evidence-only plans in a row. The progress guard from the re-plan table: 194 and 195 must each change rendered UI structure (extraction) or add a checkable state; the spike must not be followed by a second evidence-only plan.
- Deleting the `phase173` fixture mode before 194 stops using it (the nightly and `phase173_*` specs read it).

## Risks
1. The spike numbers fire a trigger and the milestone forks mid-flight (mitigated by the go/no-go rule and by 193 being storybook-independent: fixtures and the lint do not need the dependency).
2. The extraction is large for 2,055 and 2,490 line LiveViews; the pilot only touches the three JOB-03 surfaces (263, 725 and part of 1,253 lines) and must not drift into Search or Sync and index.
3. Required-lane cost grows: cold `ops-ui` over +3 minutes is itself a trigger.
4. The retained preview refresh surprises (dirty checkout, reseed). Use preflight and the seed-disabled override; keep the maintainer's untracked `my-search.json` untouched.
5. The 10 of 12 budget is consumed by adding rather than replacing rows.
6. Fidelity gaps in System mode or in host hook registration (`ops` JS hooks must be mirrored in `js_path`; memory note on host-app registration).

## Confidence and what would change it
Confidence 0.72 that "four phases, spike-first, go/no-go, lint replaces the table" is the right next milestone. It falls to 0.5 if the maintainer wants adopter features sooner than the screens work (choose option 3 then, and keep fixtures 193 as a standalone cleanup), if the nightly `ecommerce-e2e` is still red for a non-flake reason, or if the first look at `mix hex.audit` for the storybook subtree shows an open advisory. It rises to 0.85 if the spike lands under 1 day of CSS work and a warm cold-delta below 3 minutes. Unknown (U): the actual cold NIF cost, whether the iframe System mode is faithful, and the true size of the Failed sync list extraction.

## Adversarial pass (arguing against the recommendation)
- "A catalog is a human-review tool, and the owner wants no human review; this is investing in the wrong thing." Partly right: browsing is not evidence. What survives: the catalog earns its cost only as a host for executable checks (render-all, lint, citation, axe, pairs). That is why 193 and the lint come before any story count growth, and why 196 is cut by default. If the checks cannot be hosted without the dependency, the fallback harness is cheaper.
- "The copy table already guards the copy and the Phase 190 work just finished; the catalog duplicates it." Mostly survives only because the table renders 16 LiveView states and cannot reach states with no fixture surface (`not_covered` in `copy_rules.json`). If the lint cannot reach more states than the 16 after 193, delete 194's copy goal and keep the table.
- "Adopter features are the project's core value and are blocked behind internal tooling." Strong. What survives: nothing in the catalog blocks the library; the milestone is 8 to 10 plans, and the maintainer can pick option 3 at no loss, provided fixtures consolidation (193) is kept because it removes production fixture branches. I would flag this as the decision for the maintainer, with a default of the catalog because ADR 0001 and 0002 were already decided and the prep is done.
- "phoenix_storybook is a dependency with one maintainer and a Rust NIF, against the house rule." Survives with the fallback and triggers; the spike is exactly the place to fail cheaply. The strongest version of the objection is that the spike will almost certainly go (ADR decided, no advisory, version supported), so the hand-rolled harness never gets priced against real numbers. Mitigation: record the fallback LOC estimate against the spike's actual effort.
- "Extraction pixel parity is claimed by structural pairs, which are calibrated on a recolor only." Survives as a limit: a pair check proves structure, copy and state identity, not pixels; say so in every summary.
- "Eight to ten plans is optimistic; v1.44 had 17 and v1.46 had 16." Plausible. The pilot extraction and `decide/4` design are the likely overruns; the cut line (196, and the second half of 195's stories) absorbs it. If 194 alone needs 4 plans, stop and re-plan rather than extend.

What survives overall: spike-first with a number-based fork, consolidation instead of new build for fixtures and copy, replacement instead of addition for gates, and a default-cut 196.
