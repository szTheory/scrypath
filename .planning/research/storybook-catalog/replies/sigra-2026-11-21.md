## Review loop, data, process

- Tasklane seeds fictional `.test` accounts with real differences in organization scope, sign-in status and audit history; its support flow names the actor and checks the return context. Use the same principle: one credible store, one failed item, a coherent timeline and an operator with a reason to act. Ordinary data on the opening story; long values, empty results and failures get their own links. Pin preview event times and evaluate connected expiry or recency explicitly. "One unique state per adopter" is a useful selection heuristic only if the adopter changes what the reviewer needs to see or do.
- Review: a stable seeded URL and an operator task; record story, actor, case, theme, viewport and observed issue; group fixes by owning component; keep human review for whether the task reads clearly and the next action makes sense. **Sigra's v1.50 warning:** a large evidence pipeline produced zero comparable samples and two verified phases out of eight. Keep cheap preflights, exact-source verification and one negative control where it catches a consequential regression; drop duplicate collectors; investigate the failed prerequisite before repeating an expensive run.

## Biggest risk and the advice

- **Biggest risk (high):** weeks spent building a catalog nobody can use to review one realistic operator decision. Early warning: a growing grid or screenshot inventory before the owner has a stable link and can say which failed item they are looking at, what they can do and how they know recovery worked.
- **First five (all high):** (1) pick one job and write its three cases (routine path, actionable error, real denial or stale-confirmation boundary), naming actor, scope, record and next step, starting from the existing recovery fixture; (2) prove a thin dev mount: one production control and one full composition with the real CSS, time a cold build, prove production exclusion; (3) one shared fixture source with fixed preview time, preserved ids and history across steps and an assertion for the intentional change; (4) one mounted journey asserting the restricted action leaves state unchanged and the permitted recovery affects the selected record; (5) put it in front of the owner on the stable preview and fix what the review exposes.
- **Refuse for the pilot (all high):** a universal state grid or manifest framework; hash-bound screenshots or a full width-by-theme matrix; a second gallery beside Storybook (require replacement proof before retiring an old lane); a paid LLM critic as a gate; framework upgrades, copied brand assets or dependency exceptions bundled into the pilot.
- **What sigra wishes someone had said (high): test the handoff between two apparently good screens.** Assert who the viewer is, which record they are on, which facts carry forward, which field the action changes and where they return; individual screenshots and a green final-state assertion miss this class (accrue's intermediate refund mismatch; threadline's admin journey versus support denial).
- **Ten rules for a small team:** start with one operator job; use one coherent fictional adopter; render production components and assets; give a case a stable link; keep ordinary data easy to find; name errors and boundaries that change the next step; check identities and state across transitions; prove authorization and writes in the mounted host; keep Storybook out of the shipped artifact; get human feedback on the whole job before expanding coverage.
