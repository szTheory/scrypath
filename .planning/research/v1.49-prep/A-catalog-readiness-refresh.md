> **Superseded in part (2026-11-21).** The maintainer's feedback session called deferring the catalog "a huge miss" and said they want the storybook now. The catalog is the lead candidate for the next milestone: see [FEEDBACK-SESSION-2026-11-21](../storybook-catalog/FEEDBACK-SESSION-2026-11-21.md) and the fold-in at the top of `.planning/MILESTONE-CONTEXT.md`. ## Recommendation in short

**Shrink it. v1.49 becomes "Fixtures and catalog spike": two phases, about 4 plans, ending at a recorded go/no-go. The pilot (read-only half of JOB-03, `decide/4`) and "catalog at scale" are not part of v1.49; they become a separate decision the maintainer makes after reading the spike numbers.**

- Phase 195 Fixtures consolidation (does not need storybook): one `fixtures/scrypath_fixtures` project per ADR 0002 replaces the two drifting `Phase173FixtureSource` copies (396-line file, 144 diff lines between the copies, V) and the unreachable-in-prod `:phase173_fixture` branch in `PostureLive`. Invariant tests run inside the existing `ops-ui` lane.
- Phase 196 Spike and go/no-go: add `{:phoenix_storybook, "~> 1.5", only: [:dev, :test]}`, a dev-only loopback route, five primitives in light, dark and System, a render-all ExUnit check, and the ADR 0001 numbers (cold and warm `ops-ui`, NIF fetch, CSS isolation effort, `mix hex.audit` of the new subtree, Actions cache effect). The milestone closes there. Continuing into the pilot needs the maintainer's yes after the numbers.
- Number the phases from 195 (192 and 193 shipped in v1.48, 194 was cut and keeps its number). This is a planning-text edit only.

**Confidence 0.55.** The honest summary is that nothing in the catalog is evidence-driven; the case for doing any of it is the maintainer's standing direction (OWNER-INTENT, ADR 0001, ADR 0002) plus the fact that it needs no maintainer input to start, while the alternative (adopter features) needs an API-shape grilling first.

**What would change it:**
- Toward **deferring** the catalog (0.3 as the runner-up): the maintainer is ready to grill the five adopter-feature API shapes now. Then v1.49 is adopter features, and the fixtures consolidation can still ride as one small non-milestone PR. Also toward deferral: the next maintainer read of the traffic or an outside report says someone is evaluating the library, because every minute then belongs on the library.
- Toward the **plan as written** (0.1): the maintainer says they will actually browse the storybook as their review surface, or an outside adopter mounts ScrypathOps from source and reports an ops-UI problem.
- Toward **dropping** it (0.05): the spike trips two ADR 0001 triggers, or the maintainer reverses the 2026-10-25 decision to want a catalog at all.
- Stop inside the shrunk plan: any unpatched advisory open over 14 days on the new subtree, or a cold `ops-ui` more than 3 minutes slower, per ADR 0001.

## Options considered

### 1. As written (four phases plus a reserved fifth, 8 to 10 plans)
Spike, fixtures plus lint, pilot read-only half, pilot retry half with `decide/4`, 196 default cut.
- For: matches the approved direction; each phase has a defined check output; the go/no-go rule already stops it cheaply if the spike misfires.
- Against: it pre-commits to the pilot before any spike number exists; the pilot (extracting three screens of 263, 725 and 1,253 lines, designing `decide/4`) is the part with real cost and real overrun risk (v1.44 had 17 plans and v1.46 had 16 against estimates of about 11); the strongest reasons B gave for it have gone stale (fact 10: the a11y defect is already fixed; fact 11); and it is the third consecutive internal-UI arc (v1.46 re-skin, v1.47 brand completion, this) with no outside signal that anyone uses the ops UI.
- Verdict: not as written.

### 2. Shrunk to fixtures plus spike (recommended)
- For: delivers the two pieces that are cheap, independently useful and low-regret: fixtures consolidation (removes duplicated test code and one dead production branch) and a priced, reversible answer to the ADR 0001 question. It hosts at least one new check that exists nowhere today (render-all over primitives, route absence in prod, dev/test presence). It restores a decision point where the maintainer can choose the pilot with numbers in hand. It touches nothing adopters see, adds nothing to the Hex tarball, and leaves the gate budget at 10 or 11 of 12.
- Against: a spike with no pilot hosts few checks (B's own point). The primary payoff, area components over pure view-models and a whole-job test, is deferred. Two small milestones in a row can look like churn; the milestone ceremony (new-milestone, audit, complete) costs about as much as one plan.
- Mitigation: the cut line is the point; if the maintainer says "continue", the pilot is opened as the next milestone from the already-written B plan with updated numbers.

### 3. Defer the catalog; v1.49 is adopter features (after grilling)
- For: the only option that touches the Core Value; zero new dependency; the library is where the project's "feels complete before release" bar lives. B's adversarial pass called this "a legitimate maintainer choice, not an error".
- Against: no feature has outside evidence either (fact 1, fact 21); the grilling needs the maintainer now, and nothing is ready to build until it happens; the catalog's loose ends (a vacuous boundary test, two ledger rows pointing at "v1.48", a closed Dependabot PR #141, ADR 0001 untested) need a recorded revisit trigger anyway.
- Verdict: the runner-up. Take it if the maintainer is ready to grill API shapes at this boundary. The two options are not exclusive in time: the shrunk catalog needs no maintainer input, so the grilling can be put to the maintainer in the same boundary message and v1.50 can start from prepared shapes.

### 4. Drop the catalog
Supersede ADR 0001 and 0002, delete the storybook boundary test and the two ledger rows, delete the owner-intent research.
- For: honest to the evidence; ends the carrying cost of dead plans.
- Against: it reverses a direction the maintainer set explicitly on 2026-10-25 and re-confirmed in the 2026-11-18 re-plan, on the strength of a traffic reading that does not measure ops-UI value; it also throws away the fixtures consolidation, which is cheap and good hygiene on its own.
- Verdict: not without a maintainer statement. Revisit if the spike trips triggers.

## Lens notes

**Security.** New exposure is the playground, which evaluates HEEx templates on the server. The critical RCE in this exact feature was fixed in 1.1.0 and 1.5.0 is clear (fact 8), but the history is a signal about the attack surface, not just a fixed bug. Controls that matter: dependency `only: [:dev, :test]` (already boundary-tested statically), route compiled in dev only (not test, no prod), loopback publish when run in Compose, a prod-compile assertion that no storybook module or route exists, and `mix hex.audit` of the new subtree recorded by hand because `deep-quality` is already red for the sigra-to-cloak_ecto cause and would hide a new finding. Footgun: `deps.get` in the boundary test's fresh consumer pulls the NIF on a cold cache; rustler_precompiled verifies a checksum, but it is a compile-time download from GitHub releases, so a network blip becomes a required-lane failure. The NIF concern in B is real but small: nine packages in an unpublished app that never ships to adopters; the lesson from other ecosystems (Rails and npm devDependency incidents) is that dev-only tooling is where supply-chain surprises land, so pin and audit by hand at the spike and again if the pilot proceeds.

**Product.** Who benefits is the maintainer, plus a hypothetical host that mounts ScrypathOps. Adopters of the Hex package never see the ops UI or the catalog (facts 2 and 3). That is the central objection and it is correct. The shrunk plan limits the spend to the part with a non-UI payoff (fixtures) and the part that answers a question (the spike). Lesson from other ecosystems: Primer, GOV.UK and Polaris keep catalogs because many contributors and consumers use the components; component catalogs at a bus-factor-one project rot unless stories feed tests, which is why the render-all gate must exist before the first story PR merges.

**Architecture.** Keep the boundary: catalog and `.story.exs` outside `lib/`, fixtures in their own dev/test-only path project, nothing in the published package. The deep-module idea (`decide/4` as one pure function with a closed outcome enum) is good design but belongs to the pilot, not to this plan. Do not migrate `CopySurfaces` to stories (it tests the mount path). Fixture consolidation is safe independent of storybook (B's own conclusion, kept). Footgun: deleting the `phase173` production branch before the e2e specs and `ui-preservation-states.ts` stop using it breaks the nightly; move the specs to the new source first.

**DevOps and CI.** Wall time added to the PR critical path is about zero unless the NIF fails (fact 16). Cost is cold-cache time and cache pressure on a cache already over its cap (fact 15), so the spike should record the cache effect of the new subtree. The browser sweep stays out of hosted PR lanes. Gate budget: 10 of 12 used (fact 4, not 8). The shrunk plan adds at most one row (fixture invariants, if counted conservatively as its own enforcement file), reaching 11. The lint replacing the copy table row only happens in the pilot; until then the two rows keep their deletion triggers, and their "v1.48" wording should be corrected to "the catalog milestone that ingests `copy_rules.json`" so they do not trigger on the wrong number. Pre-push confidence: the plan's checks (`mix verify.ops_ui`, the boundary test, `mix verify.package`) are runnable locally; `ops-ui` is about 390 s hosted and builds cold temporary graphs, so a local run is a long one; run the cheap boundary and package checks first.

**Design/UX and accessibility.** Under the zero-human-review rule, a browsable catalog is not evidence, and nothing here proves a screen looks right or that hierarchy and voice are good; those stay maintainer decisions and are never described as reviewed. The only machine-checkable a11y value is axe over primitives rendered alone (attributable results), and even that is advisory until clean. The one a11y defect B wanted the catalog to find was fixed without it (fact 10). No real screen-reader coverage exists or is claimed. The owner's bar (both themes, typography, hierarchy, mobile first) is served better by the pilot's extraction than by the spike, which is the argument for leaving the pilot open as a live option instead of dropping it.

**OSS maintainer and adopter.** Adopter value of the shrunk plan is none directly (nothing ships). Maintainer value: a cleaner fixture source, one less dead production branch, and a decided dependency question instead of a latent one. Bus factor: phoenix_storybook is one person plus dependabot; the cadence is healthy (1.0.0 in March, five releases to 1.5.0 in September, pushes in November), and the unpublished boundary plus ADR fallback caps the downside. The house rule ("copy-paste beats a dependency, flat trees") is already traded away by ADR 0001 for the browse surface; the spike is where that trade meets a real number. Judgment (not verified against other projects): heavy dev tooling is safer in a separate Mix project than in the main tree; B's "make the catalog its own Mix project" fallback is the right escape and is already pre-agreed.

## Adversarial pass (the strongest case against the recommendation)

1. **"This is the milestone that should not exist."** Every adopter-facing and evidence-based signal says the audience is about one person, and the catalog serves the ops UI, which adopters never see. A shrunk catalog is still internal tooling, and the v1.48 rule ("when the release train is idle and there is no approved work item, say so plainly instead of inventing work") reads as an argument for saying "idle". Reply: it is an approved work item (direction of 2026-10-25, re-confirmed 2026-11-18, ADRs written), not an invented one; and the shrunk plan is deliberately the smallest slice that leaves the maintainer able to say stop. But if the maintainer's view is that the whole UI-engine arc is now over-investment, the cleanest response is option 3 or 4, and I would not argue hard against it.
2. **"A two-plan spike answers a question nobody is asking."** The spike will almost certainly go (ADR decided, no open advisory, version supported), so the fallback harness is never priced and the milestone yields a green dependency add and little else. B made the same point. Reply: that is a reason to record the fallback's cost estimate in the spike, not to skip it; and the fixtures phase has independent value.
3. **"Fixtures consolidation does not need a milestone."** True. A 396-line move plus an invariant test and one deleted branch could be a single non-milestone PR. If the maintainer prefers fewer milestone boundaries, do exactly that and skip the spike until a pilot is wanted. That would be option 3 with a small PR, and it costs almost nothing to choose later.
4. **"The cost model is too kind."** I argue wall time stays flat because `ops-ui` is off the critical path. That depends on `ecommerce-mounted` staying at 824 s or more; if a later speed pass shortens it below the new `ops-ui` time, the catalog's cold-cache cost becomes the critical path. And the NIF download recurring on a cold 10.77 GB cache is unmeasured (U). The spike's numbers are the correction.
5. **"Milestone ceremony is itself the waste."** A 4-plan milestone with audit, complete and archive costs close to one extra plan's worth of procedure. Value-over-procedure says fold it. Reply: fold it if the maintainer agrees; the GSD lifecycle is the standing instruction (2026-10-25) and this is the maintainer's call.

**What evidence would flip it.**
- To option 3: the maintainer will do the grilling now, or any outside signal about the library appears (a referrer that repeats, an issue, a PR, a star from a non-maintainer account that opens an issue).
- To option 1: a named person uses the ops UI from source; or the maintainer commits to browsing stories as their regular review path (then the human-browse payoff is real).
- To option 4: the spike trips two ADR 0001 triggers, or the maintainer says the UI engine is over.
- Within option 2: if the fixture consolidation needs more than 2 plans, stop and re-plan; if the spike's cold `ops-ui` rises by more than 3 minutes, switch to the separate-Mix-project escape or the hand-rolled harness as ADR 0001 says.
