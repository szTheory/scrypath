Scrypath milestone ratchet and roadmap guide
============================================

Purpose
-------

Use this guide when reassessing Scrypath's direction or starting a new GSD
milestone. Preserve the intent: build and maintain an exceptionally dependable,
ergonomic Elixir search library while spending effort only where evidence says
it will help adopters. Do not turn the existence of a roadmap into a reason to
keep shipping work.

This guide is a decision aid, not an active milestone, release commitment, or
promise that every candidate will be built. The current milestone state,
requirements, and approved scope remain authoritative in GSD planning.

Product anchor
--------------

Scrypath is an Ecto-native, Phoenix-friendly, Meilisearch-first library for
indexing and search orchestration. Its core value is to make indexing feel
native to Ecto without hiding the operational realities of keeping search in
sync. Preserve explicit sync, deletion, backfill, reindex, tenant, and failure
semantics. Keep the runtime framework-agnostic, retain the internal adapter
seam, and do not promise a public multi-backend abstraction without adoption
evidence.

Consider the people and workflows directly relevant to each proposed change:

- Phoenix/Ecto application developers adopting, composing, and debugging search.
- Operators diagnosing indexing, backend, queue, drift, and recovery states.
- Library maintainers protecting compatibility, release trust, supportability,
  security, and contributor flow.
- Realistic adopter examples that exercise the shared integration boundaries.

Use domain or industry variation when it changes a concrete workflow or failure
mode. Do not multiply personas, industries, APIs, or fixtures just to make a
matrix look comprehensive. Choose representative adopters and boundary cases
that maximize useful coverage.

Portfolio posture and horizons
------------------------------

Use evidence-gated horizons, not calendar commitments. Refresh these candidates
after each milestone and remove stale or completed ideas rather than preserving
an ever-growing backlog.

Current position (2026-09-26): v1.39 is complete and archived; its readiness
decision is NOT READY because conditions 3 and 6 remain UNKNOWN. The dated
assessment is preserved as historical evidence. The final exact-SHA closeout
later passed and tag `v1.39` points to that final commit, so condition 6 can be
reassessed with a new dated record. No next milestone is approved. See
`.planning/reference/milestone-candidates.md` for the current evidence-gated
near, mid, and long horizons.

NEAR TERM — maintenance and trust

- Keep `main` green on the lean required checks; prefer PR-first execution for
  serious changes and close each change with a clean worktree and truthful
  planning/release records.
- Release when a warranted change is ready. Do not create a release or milestone
  merely to maintain activity.
- Keep package, compatibility, support, docs, adopter proof, and CI truth aligned.
- Act on concrete bugs, security issues, release compatibility, proof drift, or
  reviewed adopter evidence. Otherwise say plainly that the release train is
  idle.

MID TERM — owner-approved, evidence-backed wedges

- Scrypath's non-UI engineering quality ratchet reached its measured
  diminishing-return boundary in v1.37: no confirmed compatible high- or
  medium-leverage finding remained open. Do not schedule another broad cleanup
  or optimization pass without new evidence.
- The v1.39 readiness assessment is complete, but conditions 3 and 6 remain
  UNKNOWN. A narrowly scoped, automated evidence follow-up is a candidate only
  if the owner approves it; first inspect whether existing evidence can answer
  the question without new product work or duplicated checks.
- ScrypathOps already received focused design-system, operator-flow, and
  accessibility/theme work in v1.32–v1.34. The owner wants to return to
  operator UI after the non-UI diminishing-return gate passes, but does not
  currently have time for it. Treat it as a deferred candidate, not an
  approved milestone.
- Reopen product work when reviewed adopter evidence, a concrete production bug,
  release compatibility pressure, or an explicit owner-approved strategic
  decision identifies a material gap.

LONG TERM — preserve options without promising breadth

- Reassess larger capabilities only when multiple real workflows establish
  demand and the cost of preserving the current product boundary exceeds the
  cost and complexity of expansion.
- Public backend broadening, autocomplete/suggestions, vector or hybrid search,
  personalization, new public runtime API categories, and reusable UI/product
  surfaces remain gated by the project's scope authority and strong evidence.
- If no candidate clears those gates, remaining idle is a successful decision.

How to choose a milestone
------------------------

Before writing requirements, state the evidence, the adopter/operator job that
is blocked, why this is the right time, the smallest useful outcome, and what
will explicitly remain out of scope. Compare plausible approaches across
developer experience, operations/SRE, security/privacy, compatibility,
maintenance, and product scope. Fan out research or specialist review only when
independent perspectives can change the decision enough to justify their cost.

Use the smallest vertical slice that proves the outcome. Prefer simple, idiomatic
Elixir and Ecto patterns, clear module boundaries, explicit failure behavior,
readable names, acyclic dependencies, and self-documenting code. Write
adopter-facing documentation in plain, task-focused language and keep it accurate
to supported behavior. Avoid speculative abstractions,
duplicate sources of truth, cleanup unrelated to the approved change, and code
or tests whose maintenance cost exceeds their signal.

Do not create a milestone solely to process routine Dependabot PRs or ordinary
dependency churn. Handle routine updates through the normal maintenance/release
flow. A security or compatibility event can justify a focused milestone when
its impact, coupled work, and proof needs exceed a small maintenance change.

Verification and CI
-------------------

Default to zero human verification or UAT for software behavior. Convert each
acceptance claim into executable evidence before implementation: unit/property,
contract, seam, integration, smoke, browser/accessibility, or exact-SHA hosted
proof, selecting the cheapest reliable layer that exercises the relevant
boundary. Include meaningful happy paths, error cases, and boundary conditions.

Shift checks left while implementing. Put recurring checks in CI only when their
repeat confidence and risk reduction justify their runtime and maintenance cost.
Keep expensive or service-backed proof scheduled or advisory when that provides
useful recurring confidence without burdening every change; promote it to a
required gate only when evidence warrants it. Reuse harnesses and services,
avoid duplicated test execution, use bounded representative compatibility
matrices, and give failures useful diagnostics, isolation, timeouts, and cleanup.
Optimize total CI feedback time and GitHub Actions minutes together: run cheap,
deterministic checks before costly service-backed work where dependencies allow;
name jobs for the capability they prove; make logs and failure summaries
actionable; remove tautological, duplicate, or low-signal checks instead of
keeping them for coverage optics.
Use property-based testing for high-risk input spaces and invariants where it
adds coverage that examples do not cheaply provide; avoid property suites without
a named failure mode or decision value.

Treat performance as a standing quality lens, not permission for speculative
optimization. Reuse reproducible baselines, identify the largest adopter-visible
or resource-cost bottlenecks, and optimize measured CPU, memory, network, and I/O
costs when the expected gain exceeds implementation and maintenance cost. Record
the before/after evidence and any tradeoff.

Use a digital-twin adopter only to exercise realistic end-to-end boundaries that
are not already proved more cheaply. Keep its scenarios deterministic and
representative; do not mistake synthetic success for outside-adopter validation.
Human input is reserved for irreducible product decisions, external credentials
or permissions, or physical-world behavior that automation cannot observe.

Release and repository hygiene
------------------------------

Ship when warranted and evidence is ready. For release-bound milestones, triage
PRs, require the agreed checks on the exact candidate/final commit, confirm
post-merge `main` is green, follow the documented package/docs publication
sequence, and record package-to-source parity. If a release prerequisite blocks,
report release-ready with the exact blocker and resume action; do not call it
shipped. Remove task-owned branches, worktrees, service stacks, temporary files,
and generated artifacts; preserve unrelated user changes. Keep docs accurate,
planning artifacts discoverable, and historical evidence immutable unless an
explicit correction is needed.
Keep implementation comments and public docs free of transient GSD plan chatter
or task bookkeeping. Put durable decisions in planning records and remove only
the artifacts owned by the completed work.

Privacy and durable context
---------------------------

Never commit personal data, private credentials, environment dumps, or identifying
machine paths. Keep secrets out of logs, fixtures, telemetry, and artifacts. If
local comparisons need fingerprints, use non-reversible checksums only where
appropriate and keep sensitive source material local. Follow twelve-factor
configuration practices: configuration comes from the environment or explicit
deployment configuration, not hard-coded machine-specific values.

Record durable decisions, evidence, and provenance in the relevant `.planning`
source of truth. Prefer updating or replacing stale guidance over accumulating
competing copies. Before recommending a next GSD command, ensure the needed
intent, decisions, evidence, and scope are saved in the artifact that command
will read; never rely on conversational context surviving a reset.

Pre-operator UI readiness
------------------------

Before treating non-UI quality work as complete, use the durable pre-operator UI
readiness program in `.planning/reference/PRE-OPERATOR-UI-READINESS.md`. Reuse
existing evidence, assess every named product-quality dimension, and close or
explicitly disposition all critical, high, and medium-leverage findings. Only
then recommend **READY FOR OPERATOR UI**. The v1.39 assessment is complete and
NOT READY; its condition 3 evidence gaps still need a new, decision-relevant
assessment. Condition 6's historical UNKNOWN is not rewritten, though the later
final closeout evidence is available for a newly dated reassessment. ScrypathOps
is a possible later focus only after the gate passes and maintainer time is
available. This does not authorize capabilities prohibited by the current scope
guard.

Closeout and roadmap refresh
----------------------------

At each milestone close, reconcile requirements, roadmap, project state,
candidate list, release truth, and retrospective lessons. Record what shipped,
what evidence changed the strategy, which candidates were removed or deferred,
and the next evidence gate. Do not manufacture a next milestone when none is
approved. Refresh the machine-readable STATE and current roadmap summary without
rewriting archived assessments. Before recommending a next GSD command, make
sure the intent and relevant source evidence are durable so clearing context
does not lose the thread.
